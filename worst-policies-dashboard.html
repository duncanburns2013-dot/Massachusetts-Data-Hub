Energy — the most completely documented chain in the repos

Statute → charge → line item → roll call. Every mandate below has a bill number, a section, and a recorded vote.

The structural finding: Massachusetts law does not merely permit costs to be passed to ratepayers — in Ch. 169/2008 §83C and St. 2024 c. 239 §83E it guarantees it. §83C codifies "full cost recovery" for offshore wind PPAs and awards utilities up to 2.75% remuneration on contracts they did not have to negotiate down. The 2024 storage mandate carrying the same guarantee is titled "An Act Promoting a Clean Energy Grid, Advancing Equity and Protecting Ratepayers."

H.5151 — the process, not just the content. An 85-section energy bill dropped by House Ways & Means on Feb 24, 2026. Representatives had 24 hours to read it and file amendments, 48 hours to the floor vote. Healey's original was 45 sections with 90 days for amendments and 6 hours of public hearings — H.5151 is 89% larger with 99% less review time. The advertised "$9 billion over 10 years" works out to $310/yr per household; Mass-Fiscal-Energy-Legislation puts realistic near-term relief at ~$150/yr, closing about 15% of the gap to the US average. It also stripped Healey's proposal to ban recovery of utility lobbying costs from ratepayers.

The MBTA is a separate balance sheet with the same disease. Pension 52.6% funded ($1.62B unfunded), $473M–$700M annual operating deficit as federal aid expires, a $13B capital backlog, and $8B+ in Big Dig highway debt transferred onto a transit agency in 2000. Combined shortfall ~$1.1B/yr = $412 per Massachusetts household. MBTA-Scam costs out ten reforms totalling +$1,100M/yr that require no new taxes — including removing the Big Dig debt (+$200M) and fixing the sales-tax formula (+$300M).

Housing — subsidizing demand into a supply shortage

The through-line across four repos: spending has shifted decisively toward subsidizing demand for existing units rather than producing new ones.

MBTA Communities Act § 3A. 177 communities must zone for 273,080 units of multifamily capacity near transit — 15.2% of the state's entire 2020 housing stock — with no affordability requirement. Applying the state's own DCAMM fee schedule, Brensley's analysis for the State Auditor puts designer base fees alone at ~$4.49B. The state appropriated $15M (Catalyst Fund) — a 99.7% unfunded mandate, before land, infrastructure, permitting, financing, or municipal compliance staffing.

EOHLC compliance model v1.5 (177 communities, 273,080 units); Brensley/DCAMM fee methodology; Catalyst Fund appropriation. Via MBTA-Communities-Act. Log scale — the bars are 300× apart.

The $25K subsidy inflates exactly the tier it targets

Zillow ZHVI price tiers, Jan 2019 → Mar 2026, via The-25K-Problem. In Gateway Cities the bottom tier (5th–35th percentile — the subsidy-eligible band) rose 74% against 57% at the top: a 17-point gap. In non-Gateway communities the same gap is 5 points.

Why $25K makes it worse, not better. The award covers 167% of the FHA down payment on a $300K home and 50% on a $1M home — so the demand boost concentrates in the cheapest markets. It is a deferred second mortgage, repayable at sale or refinance. When every buyer at a price point receives it, no individual gains an advantage and the price absorbs it: on a $350K home the price inflates to ~$370K while the buyer owes $376K in total debt. Negative equity on day one. Massachusetts has 1.6 months of inventory; a balanced market needs 4–6.

Transparency — the architecture, not the loophole

Two structural exemptions and one legal loophole make the rest of this dashboard harder to audit than it should be.

Question 1, November 2024: 71.8% of voters gave the State Auditor authority to audit the Legislature. Legislative leaders refused to comply and hired outside counsel to fight it. On February 10, 2026, Auditor DiZoglio filed a complaint with the Supreme Judicial Court to enforce the vote. The-Peoples-Audit exists because the formal audit was refused.

Healthcare lobbying — the top 5 systems, 2020–2025 ($M)

MA Secretary of State lobbyist registrations via Pay-To-Play-Massachusetts-Non-Profits. $39M industry total. Mass General Brigham's and Cambridge Health Alliance's CEOs are personally registered lobbyists. CHA draws 75% of its funding from government and spent $1.6M lobbying that government. The Mass Health & Hospital Association trade group outspent every individual hospital at $6.3M.

The $200 pattern. OCPF raw-data analysis in pay-to-play-dashboard finds donations clustering at exactly the $200 statutory lobbyist cap — MGB VP Christopher Philbin made 28 donations in 2024, every one at exactly $200. Meanwhile the #4 lobbying firm by earnings (O'Neill & Partners, $13.6M) cannot be searched in OCPF at all; the system returns an error. HHS-MA-DOGE traces the same circuit through Medicaid: registered lobbyists donating to the legislators they are paid to influence, union PAC payroll deductions harvested from PCAs ($2–$18 per paycheck into a $14.09M 1199SEIU PAC), a hospital trade-association pass-through PAC, and $6.6M+ in no-bid migrant shelter contracts to a provider whose CEO donated to the Governor.

Education, health and verification

MCAS proficiency before and after the graduation requirement

DESE MCAS results via education-statewide and MA-v-NH. Voters repealed the MCAS graduation requirement in November 2024 (Question 2). In 2025 — the first year without it — every single student group's Grade 10 scores fell. UMass Donahue attributes 84% of MCAS score variance to socioeconomic factors, so the level was never the whole story; the universal one-year drop is.

2026 health premium increases vs. the state's own benchmark

MA Division of Insurance finalized decisions, July 2025, via healthcare-dashboard. The state cost-growth benchmark is 3.6%. DOI did reject BCBS (12.9%) and WellSense (16.2%) as excessive and negotiated $54M in savings — every approved rate still lands at 2–3.4× the benchmark. MA already carries the nation's highest family premium at $28,151 (+14.7% vs US).

Benefits fraud — detected vs. modeled (SNAP, $M/yr)

MA State Auditor BSI reports vs. USDA-FNS trafficking studies, via fraud-detection and affordability-dashboard. BSI examines roughly 1,500–2,000 cases against ~2.2M benefit recipients — 0.07% coverage. Of examined cases, 6.6% showed fraud.

Boston: equity apparatus vs. the departments that deliver

City of Boston ACFR General Fund actual expenditures and data.boston.gov payroll, via All-Things-Boston. DEI/equity departments went $548K → $28.5M (52×) and 46 → 429 staff while BPD lost 177 officers and Veterans Services stayed flat at $2.5M–$3.3M. The four neighborhoods absorbing 80% of Boston's shootings also wait 3–4× longer for 311 resolution — Mattapan 11.2 days vs. Back Bay 3.1.

Verification gaps in the shelter system, pre-2025. No criminal background checks except in overflow, no comprehensive immigration-status verification, limited residency verification, self-reported income accepted with no IRS, SSA or employment-database cross-check, and no cross-state residency check. The "one child loophole": if any family member has legal status the entire family qualifies. At $3,496–$3,870 per family per week, a single fraudulent placement is worth ~$200,000/year. The February 2025 $425M supplemental fixed most of this — six-month cap, hard 4,000-family ceiling, residency + lawful status + CORI verification, competitive bidding, 200% FPL income cap. Caseload fell 73% and cost per family fell to $1,182/week. The reforms work; the question the repos raise is why they took until 2025.

Method, source repositories, and where the data disagrees

What this dashboard is. A synthesis. Every figure here already existed in one of the 29 Massachusetts Data Hub research repositories, sourced there to a primary government document. Nothing was newly researched or estimated for this page. The ranking is the only new judgment: entries are ordered by the size and directness of the documented dollar cost, and that ordering reflects the repos' own framing and figures.
Where the repos disagree with each other. These are unreconciled and should be treated as ranges, not point estimates:
  • FY2024 shelter spending: $856M (Boston Herald, via The-Invasion) · $894M (immigration-dashboard) · $932M (MA-v-NH) · $978M (MASTER_DATA.md). Spread: $122M.
  • Cost per shelter family per week: $3,496 (State Auditor, MASTER_DATA.md) vs. $3,870 (fraud-detection) vs. ~$10,000/month (Senate Ways & Means, Massachusetts-Disaster).
  • MA electricity premium over the national average: +56% (EIA Apr 2026, energy-dashboard) · +74% (EIA Nov 2024, ma-electricity-analysis) · +79% (EIA Dec 2025, Mass-Fiscal-Energy-Legislation) · +57% (Maura-Healey-Year-3-). Different EIA months, moving supply costs — but the pages don't all say so.
  • Industrial rate premium: +112% (energy-dashboard, EIA Oct 2025) vs. +134% (affordability-dashboard).
  • Unfunded OPEB: $13.7B (Commonwealth only) · $15.77B (Commonwealth + MBTA) · $52.8B (all entities statewide). All three are correct for different scopes; the labels don't always say which.
  • Total state liabilities: $107.6B (ACFR FY2024) vs. $122.5B (Maura-Healey-Year-3-, Dec 2025 vintage).
