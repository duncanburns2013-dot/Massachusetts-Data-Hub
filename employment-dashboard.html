MA (April): +8,500 jobs · 4.7% UR · +21,600 jobs since Sept 2025 · National +172K · 4.3%

✅ Six Straight Months of MA Job Growth

+8,500 jobs in April 2026, after a revised +7,200 in March (revised up from +6,800). Since September 2025, Massachusetts has added +21,600 payroll jobs (incl. +19,600 private) and has outpaced the country over the past six months. Source: BLS CES via Mass.gov EOLWD, released May 22, 2026.

⚠️ Unemployment Holds at 4.7% as Labor Force Continues to Shrink

MA unemployment held at 4.7% in April — 0.4 pts above the national 4.3%. Labor force fell another −11,700 in April (9,600 fewer employed, 2,200 fewer unemployed). EOLWD attributes the decline primarily to retirements; the prime-age (25–54) participation rate is 86.7%, a 10-year high.

MA vs. National Unemployment Rate (2024–2026)

📐

Why the rate can hold steady while the labor force shrinks: U-3 is a fraction — unemployed ÷ labor force. When people exit the labor force (retire, stop searching, leave the country), both denominator and numerator can fall, leaving the rate flat. EOLWD's framing is that retirements are the dominant driver and prime-age engagement is actually at a 10-year high. The supply-side story matters as much as the unemployment rate for understanding labor market tightness.

Labor Force Monthly Change — Acceleration (2025–2026)

📊

U-3 vs U-6 — what each measures: U-3 (4.7%) counts only people actively job-searching. U-4 adds discouraged workers. U-5 adds all marginally attached. U-6 (7.2%) adds involuntary part-time workers — people who want full-time work but can only find part-time. The 2.9-point gap between U-3 and U-6 represents roughly 114,000 additional MA residents who are underemployed or have given up. Note: state U-6 is published quarterly by BLS as 4-quarter moving averages. Latest available: Q3 2025.

🇺🇸 Foreign-Born Labor Force Has Stopped Growing — and the Gap is Closing

From April 2025 to April 2026, the foreign-born civilian labor force fell −153K (33.0M → 32.85M) while the native-born labor force fell −949K (137.6M → 136.7M). It's the first year-over-year decline in foreign-born labor force since the 2020 COVID shock — a hot topic given immigration policy shifts. The native-born decline is 6× larger in absolute terms, but the foreign-born decline is the bigger narrative shift. Source: BLS CPS Table A-7 (May 8, 2026 release, NSA).
