4.7% headline masks accelerating labor force collapse — 52,000 residents dropped out in 12 months

🔻 The Headline is Misleading — The Labor Force is Collapsing

MA unemployment held at 4.7% in March 2026 — not because hiring improved, but because 15,027 people left the labor force in March alone, the worst single-month drop on record. Peak to March 2026: −52,339 people exited the MA labor force in 12 months. When the denominator shrinks, the rate stays flat even if zero jobs are created.

MA vs. National Unemployment Rate (2024–2026)

⚠️

Why the rate looks stable while the labor market deteriorates: When discouraged workers stop searching, they exit the labor force entirely and disappear from the U-3 calculation. MA lost 15,027 from the labor force in March alone — if even half had stayed and been counted as unemployed, the rate would have been ~5.1% instead of 4.7%. The rate is a fraction. Both the numerator and denominator are moving.

U-3 vs. U-6 — MA Labor Underutilization (Quarterly, 4-Qtr Avg)

Labor Force Monthly Change — Acceleration (2025–2026)

📊

U-3 vs U-6 — what each measures: U-3 (4.7%) counts only people actively job-searching. U-4 adds discouraged workers. U-5 adds all marginally attached. U-6 (7.2%) adds involuntary part-time workers — people who want full-time work but can only find part-time. The 2.9-point gap between U-3 and U-6 represents roughly 114,000 additional MA residents who are underemployed or have given up. Note: state U-6 is published quarterly by BLS as 4-quarter moving averages. Latest available: Q3 2025.

⚠️ National Household Survey — Same Divergence as MA

The payroll survey (employer-based) shows +115K jobs. The household survey (worker-based) shows employed persons fell 226,000 in April and −1,034,000 since September 2025 nationally. National labor force also shrinking: −92,000 in April. This mirrors the MA pattern exactly — the 4.3% rate holds not because hiring is strong but because workers are exiting the labor force. National U-6 (true underemployment) hit 8.2% in April, up from 8.0% in March.
