What that means for the headline

The figures on this page are appropriation and payment records: what the legislature set aside and what the Comptroller paid out. They are not a total cost of immigration to Massachusetts, and nothing here should be quoted as one. Health coverage and food assistance are real costs that sit behind a disclosure wall, and the honest thing to report is the wall.

If every cost were public, what would it come to?

The records on this site stop at appropriations and payments. This is the part they cannot reach — built from a sourced household budget, with the two contested numbers left as dials you can move.

This is an estimate, and it is not a record. Everything on the other tabs is the Commonwealth's own accounting. Nothing here is. It multiplies a published household budget by a household count that no public record provides, so treat the output as arithmetic you can audit, not as a figure the state has confirmed. The inputs are deliberately exposed: move them and watch the answer move.

What this does and does not say

It says: a household in Massachusetts costs a known amount per year, and if you multiply that by a count, you get a total. Both halves are stated.

It does not say: that the taxpayer pays all of it, that every household in the count is in any particular immigration status, or that this is the Commonwealth's own figure. Households earn, and the earnings dial is there because pretending otherwise is how an estimate gets dismissed.

On the 400,000: that is a commonly cited estimate of people living in Massachusetts without legal status. It is offered as a scenario button, converted to households at the composition you pick. It is not a Massachusetts record and no state source on this site publishes it — DHS and Migration Policy Institute state pages did not resolve when this page was built on 21 October 2026. Treat it as someone's estimate, which is what it is.

How the accounts were chosen

state appropriations and actual expenses on accounts the Commonwealth names for immigrant, refugee or family-shelter purposes; recipients classified by appropriation, never by vendor name

By appropriation, never by vendor name

Accounts selected by searching appropriation_account_name for purpose words. Classifying by appropriation asks what the money was appropriated to do. A vendor-name match cannot: the prior version of this analysis caught three federally qualified health centres on the substring NEIGHBOR and a dentist on MIRA.

The lesson this page is built on. An earlier version of this analysis matched vendor names against the substrings IMMIGRANT, REFUGEE, MIRA and NEIGHBOR and reported $228.6M. Its own README documents what that produced: the three largest “immigrant vendors” were federally qualified health centres billing MassHealth for every patient, matched on NEIGHBOR; MIRA matched a dentist, a daycare and two people named Miranda. 78.4% of the total was MassHealth claims. Asking what an appropriation funds cannot make that mistake.

Reproduce it

Accounts were found by searching appropriation_account_name for the purpose words — IMMIGRAN, REFUGEE, ASYLUM, SHELTER, CITIZENSHIP, MIGRANT, RESETTLE, EMERGENCY ASSISTANCE, HOUSING TRANS. Re-run that search against CTHRU to audit the list; it is the Commonwealth's naming, not ours. Totals sum every fund row per account per year, which is what reproduces the state's own published figures.
