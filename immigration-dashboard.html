The Math

Under Biden: ~10.8M encountered nationwide. ~600K interior removals. = ~7.4M net stayed in the US.
Census Vintage 2025 (Jan 2026): NIM peaked at 2.7M in 2024, then plunged to 1.3M in 2025. Projected ~321K for 2026. US pop growth slowed to 0.5%.
Trump 2.0: FY2025 SWB encounters dropped to 237,538 — lowest since 1970 (79% below FY2024). Zero USBP releases for 7+ consecutive months.
FY2026 start: Oct+Nov 2025 = 60,940 total encounters nationwide — 28% below the previous record-low FY start (FY2012). Nov 2025 alone: 30,375 (↓92% from Biden peak of 370,883).

Who Was Removed

Biden era: ~10.8M encountered, ~600K interior removals over 4 years = 13:1 ratio.
Trump 2.0 (FY2025): MPI estimates ~340K ICE deportations — 25% above FY2024's 271K, but below Biden's combined ICE+CBP total of 685K. Interior deportations quadrupled from pre-inauguration levels. DHS claimed 2M+ "removed or self-deported" — CMS called the self-deportation figure a "self-serving fantasy."

Census Bureau Methodology Revisions — Vintage 2024 (Dec 2024) & Vintage 2025 (Jan 2026)

Vintage 2024 (Dec 2024): Added DHS, State Dept, and IIE records. Retroactive revisions showed they had undercounted NIM by 69–102% for 2021–2023.
Vintage 2025 (Jan 2026): Further refined with DOJ immigration court data. NIM peaked at 2.7M in 2024, declined to 1.3M in 2025, projected ~321K for 2026. Total 2020–2024 NIM revised to ~8.9 million — roughly double original estimates.

US Net International Migration — The Historic Nosedive

2.7M → 1.3M → 321K: NIM peaked in 2024, then collapsed. SF Fed (Nov 2025) estimates NIM may be even lower at ~500K for 2025. Both reduced immigration and increased emigration are driving the decline. Without immigration, the US working-age population would have started declining in 2012.

Legal vs Illegal — Net Fiscal Impact

Key finding: Legal immigrants are net positive at all government levels. Illegal immigrants are net negative at state/local level, where costs for education, healthcare, and shelter concentrate.
ITEP (2024): Undocumented immigrants pay $96.7B in total taxes — including $25.7B in payroll taxes to Social Security they will never collect.

Regressive Burden — Who Pays as % of Income

Bottom line: Working poor pay 2.1% of income toward immigration costs. Wealthy households pay 0.13%. The burden is deeply regressive — a 16:1 ratio. The communities bearing the heaviest costs (Chelsea, Lawrence, Lynn) also have the lowest per-capita property tax bases in Massachusetts.
