K-12 Enrollment Change (2019-2025)

Families voting with their feet: Traditional public schools lost 6.4% enrollment while homeschooling exploded 56%. Parents are fleeing a failing system.

Who Massachusetts Serves — Selected Populations, 2025-26

More than half the state is high-needs: 55.4% of Massachusetts students are classified high needs, 41.1% low income, 21.1% students with disabilities and 13.4% English learners. Any statewide average is an average of that population, not of the districts people picture.

ELA — % Meeting/Exceeding by Grade

The dip is in the middle: ELA bottoms out at 37% in Grade 4 and recovers to 45% by Grade 10, its strongest grade. No grade and subject in the state now has a majority meeting expectations.

Math — % Meeting/Exceeding by Grade

Math slides as school goes on: 42% of third graders meet expectations; by Grade 8 it is 42%. Every grade from 5 through 8 sits below where students started.

Science & Civics — Statewide

The subjects nobody reports on: Science across grades 5 and 8 sits at 41% and Civics at 40% — both below the ELA and math figures that lead every headline.

Chronic Absenteeism by Student Group, 2024-25

Nearly one student in five: 18.8% of Massachusetts students were chronically absent. Among low-income students it is 28.9% and among Hispanic students 29.5% — close to triple the 10.9% rate for Asian students.

Change from 2023-24 to 2024-25

Improving, slowly, everywhere: Every group improved, but the state moved only 0.9 points in a year. English learners improved most at 2.1 points; white students least at 0.5.

2025-26 Year to Date — Through March

MCAS Proficiency, 2022–2026

Money isn't the answer: In-district per-pupil spending rose from $19,712 to $22,414 across FY2022–FY2024, a 13.7% increase. Over the same stretch ELA proficiency moved 39%→42% and math did not move at all.

Per-Pupil Expenditure, FY2024

$23,165 per student, all in: In-district spending is $22,414; adding out-of-district placements brings the statewide total to $23,165 per pupil.

Where Students Land — Grades 3-8 ELA Achievement Levels

7% of Massachusetts students exceed expectations in ELA. 39% are only partially meeting them and 21% are not meeting them at all — so 60% of the state sits below "Meeting", the same figure the headline proficiency numbers describe from the other direction.

Graduation Gap vs. Statewide Average

Every gap runs the same direction: English learners finish 18.2 points below the state average, students with disabilities 10.6 below, low-income students 6.4 below. Asian and white students sit above it.
