Corporate Ledger

Companies and institutions with at least $1 million in headline government penalties since fiscal 2010 and a Massachusetts tie in the sources searched, beside state payments, employee donations, lobbying and officials' disclosed ties.

Private lawsuits and unconfirmed donor matches are not in it. Lobbying counts firm fees from 2010; in-house lobbying costs exist only for 2025 and 2026. Updated (loading) Method notes

Two rules behind every total

This ledger starts in fiscal 2010 (July 2009), the first year Massachusetts put its spending records online. Penalties, donations and lobbying before then are left out so every comparison covers the same years.

How penalty amounts are counted: each row counts the cash the defendant actually paid (penalties, restitution, damages, fees). Press-release headline figures are often larger because they include debt relief, forgiven debt or rent, bill credits, conditional amounts, or proposed penalties that can still be contested. Those parts are not counted here. Every one is listed under Amounts not counted, with the headline figure, what we counted, the reason and the source. Where a release does not state the split between cash and relief, the case is left out of the totals and listed there.

Two rules for duplicates and shared totals

How duplicates are counted: when two records describe the same payment, it is counted once. A record is left out only when a primary document read for this ledger shows the dollars are the same. When that document cannot be read, a strong match is still counted once and is marked 'possible duplicate, counted once until verified'. Records for different payments are all counted, even when the amounts are equal. Every record left out stays in the gross figure and is listed under Amounts not counted, with the document behind the call.

How shared totals are counted: when several defendants pay one total, each company counts only the share its primary document states. If the document gives a total and no split, and the payers include anyone outside the one company (another company, or individuals such as owners), the total is counted for no company: it stays in the gross figure and is listed under Amounts not counted. Where every payer named belongs to the one company, the whole total counts once, in full, for that company.

Counted and gross penalties

Gross is every penalty row on record. Counted leaves out a row when its dollars are already counted on another row (for example a state and a federal release announcing one settlement, or a plea and the agreement that goes with it, which the Corporate Prosecution Registry lists as two records), when it may be a duplicate that has not been verified (counted once until verified), and when its single payment is shared by several defendants and not split between them by the primary document, so each dollar is counted once and an unsplit payment counts for no company. A payment whose document states each defendant's share counts at that share. Where the two differ, the table shows gross under the counted figure. Every row left out is listed under Amounts not counted.

This is the owner's opinion. It is not a statistic and not a finding, and it follows the audited facts above rather than resting on any one of them. In the sense the Hub's pay-to-play dashboard gives the term, pay to play describes organizations that depend on state funding, lobby the officials who control that funding, and have employees who donate to those officials. The facts shown for each named person or company stay what their own records say: donated, was paid by the state, lobbied, or disclosed a tie.

What these numbers do and do not show

They show which penalized companies also appear in other public records, and in what order the dates fall.

  • They do not show that one thing caused another. Overlap is not causation: a donation, a lobbying filing or a state payment near a penalty is a correlation in the records and nothing more.
  • They do not allege that any donation or lobbying filing broke a campaign-finance or lobbying rule. That money is reported in public filings. The regulator findings (improper payments to officials and political finance) are listed under Regulator findings, with their sources.
  • Donations are gifts by individuals whose records show them working at a company. They are not payments by the company.
  • A company missing from a list may be there under a name the matching did not catch. The gaps in each record are listed with its caveats.

      How to read this picture

      The dots

      • Filled circle: a company or institution that was penalized.
      • Hollow circle: an official, or a campaign committee that received gifts.
      • Square: an agency that imposed a penalty (the SEC, the Attorney General's office), a payer of state money (CTHRU, MBTA, Massport, MSBA) or a lobbying firm.

      The lines

      • Red, solid: a penalty, between the company and the agency that imposed it.
      • Blue, dashed: money the state or one of its authorities paid the company.
      • Green, dotted: gifts to a campaign by people who list the company as their employer. They are the employees' own gifts, not company money.
      • Gold, dash-dot: lobbying fees the company paid a firm, or its own lobbying costs (recorded from 2025).
      • Pink, dashed: an official's financial disclosure that lists the company (a job, a mortgage or an investment). It carries no dollar amount.
      • A thicker line means more dollars added up across the years shown.

      Using it

      • Select a name to open its connections. Drag a dot to move it.
      • Select a line to list the records behind it, with links to the sources.
      • The year slider replays the picture from 2010, year by year.
      • A "record" is one kind of connection in one year, for example one year of state payments. "Not drawn yet" means connections held back to keep the picture readable; the button adds 40 more.

      What it does not show

      A line means a record exists in the public data. It does not say one thing caused another. Two names connected is an overlap in the records, not a finding of wrongdoing.

      Limits of this ledger
      • Donor matching uses the employer each donor reports. Donors who leave it blank or write self-employed are missed.
      • Probable (fuzzy) employer matches and matches still under review are left out of every total.
      • Lobbying firm fees start in 2010. In-house lobbying costs (a company's own staff lobbyists) are entered only for 2025 and 2026, so before 2025 a lobbying figure is firm fees only.
      • Coverage is not complete. The penalty rows come from the Corporate Prosecution Registry, EPA enforcement records and agency releases found one by one; settlements that the U.S. Attorney's Office for Massachusetts announced with companies not yet in the ledger are listed under Amounts not counted, and so is how each row was checked against its source.
      • Totals are nominal dollars.
      • Penalty amounts are the cash paid; headline figures that include relief, credits, conditional or proposed amounts are larger. Amounts not counted lists every part left out. Duplicates are counted once and shared totals follow their document; both rules are stated at the top of the page.
