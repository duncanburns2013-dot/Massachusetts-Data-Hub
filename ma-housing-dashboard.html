What’s Driving Massachusetts Home Prices?

Primary Long-Term Driver
Chronic housing supply shortage

Limited land, restrictive zoning, high construction costs, and slow new-home building have kept inventory far below demand for decades — especially on the North Shore and in Greater Boston.

Demand Amplifiers
Monetary policy (low rates, QE) + demand-side incentives

Extended low interest rates and large-scale mortgage purchases (QE) boosted buying power, especially 2020–2022. Tax incentives like the mortgage interest deduction also increase what buyers can afford, with much of the benefit capitalized into higher home prices.

Recent Trends (2026 YTD)
Modest cooling in select markets

Slightly higher inventory and longer days on market have begun to moderate price growth in Essex County and Greater Newburyport. At the same time, homeowners locked into ultra-low mortgage rates have been reluctant to sell, limiting new supply and keeping prices elevated.

Home prices have more than doubled (+108%) since 2014, driven primarily by structural supply constraints, amplified by policy-driven demand, and sustained by limited inventory even in a higher-rate environment.
